Africa Must Ask: Why Did the United States Vote Against Correcting the Map?

The General Assembly voted 164 to one to draw the world closer to its true proportions. Only Washington objected, and the question Africa should put is not how the vote went, but why it was opposed at all.

Dr. Aldo Ajou Deng-Akuey · 21 September 2026 · 2 min read

The Map of Africa, 2026: a true-scale world projection showing Africa’s actual size relative to other continents.

On 4 September 2026, the United Nations General Assembly made a historic decision. By 164 votes in favour, one against and six abstentions, the Assembly adopted the resolution entitled “Correct the Map: Rebalancing Global Cartographic Representation and Promoting Equitable Representation of the World’s Regions, Particularly Africa.” The United States was the only country to vote against it.

The resolution does not redraw Africa’s political borders, transfer territory or change the sovereignty of any African state. It concerns the way the world is visually represented. The Equal Earth projection presents the relative size of continents more accurately than the centuries-old Mercator projection, which makes Africa appear substantially smaller than it really is. Africa is approximately fourteen times the size of Greenland, although many familiar world maps visually suggest otherwise.
