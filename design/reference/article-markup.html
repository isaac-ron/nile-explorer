The first vote in over a decade

December 2026 will test whether a young nation's peace architecture can hold under democratic contestation.

By the newsroom 5 September 2026 8 min read

The election commission has published a calendar, and for the first time since independence the country has a date to organise itself around. Registration opens in the states before it opens in the capital, a sequencing choice the commission describes as practical and its critics read as political.

What follows is less a campaign than a stress test. The 2018 agreement that ended the last round of fighting distributed positions rather than settling questions: how the army is unified, how revenue is shared, who holds the courts. A vote asks each of those questions again, on a deadline.

The registration problem

Officials estimate that a majority of eligible voters have never cast a ballot. Registration drives depend on roads that close with the rains and on identity documents that many families have lost twice over, first to displacement and then to the camps. Where the paperwork is missing, the commission has said it will accept community attestation, a workable answer that will also be contested.

Party agents are already arguing about the areas where turnout will decide the outcome, and about which of those areas can realistically be reached before the calendar closes.

A vote settles who governs. It does not, on its own, settle whether the institutions holding the result are trusted.

What the region is watching

Neighbouring capitals have their own reasons to care. Oil moves north, trade moves east, and the displaced move in every direction at once. A contested result would be felt first in the border markets, where a three-week closure earlier this year cut incomes on both sides.

The commission's own budget is the quietest constraint. Ballot printing, transport and the training of poll staff are all costed in foreign currency, and the transfers that would cover them have not arrived on the schedule the calendar assumes.

None of this makes the date impossible. It makes the date a choice about what the country is willing to accept from a first attempt, and about who will be asked to live with the result.
